# Anchorage prototype

`src/game/anchoring.ts` provides a simplified training model for the chart’s Anchorage practice. It does not establish safe real-world holding. It models a single bow attachment on a uniform fictional sand bottom; it does not model chain weight, anchor type, current, bottom variation, real anchor design, or measured holding power. The force limits are gameplay tuning values, not engineering estimates.

The exercise samples tide-adjusted depth at the anchor and at points around a simplified swing circle, checks charted obstacles, and applies the rode force at the fixed simulation timestep. The player deploys from the bow, pays out, watches for set and eight seconds of holding under load, then brings the boat back and recovers the anchor. Drag or a collision interrupts holding; retry starts a new attempt. The seabed, windage and holding force are simplified, and the sampled circle cannot establish real-world clearance. The rode line is illustrative; chain catenary and seabed contact are not modelled.

The U.S. Coast Guard's [Boater's Guide to Federal Requirements for Recreational Boats](https://uscgboating.org/assets/1/AssetManager/Boaters-Guide-to-Federal-Requirements-for-Receational-Boats-20231108.pdf) recommends calculating anchor line from water depth plus bow height, with a common 5:1 to 7:1 ratio, and warns against anchoring from the stern. Scope alone does not guarantee holding; actual practice depends on conditions and equipment. The RYA's [Anchoring with Care](https://www.rya.org.uk/environment-and-sustainability/anchoring-with-care/) discusses avoiding damage to sensitive seabeds.
