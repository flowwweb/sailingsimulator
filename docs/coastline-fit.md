# Coastline fit

The coastal mesh follows the same outer basin boundary used by bathymetry. Its shoreline band has 256 segments; the source check samples 19 intermediate bearings per segment and measures less than 0.34 m of radial chord error. Compared with the previous 192-segment coast this adds 448 vertices and 768 triangles, with no new per-frame geometry or mountain meshes. Seeded shoreline rocks and stones are placed landward; the source check samples a conservative scaled bounding sphere for every instance and verifies representative footprint points against the actual high-tide incident logic.

The water shader masks only the permanent outer basin exterior; it preserves existing island handling, wave displacement and tide-dependent water shading. Source checks sample five water levels from -1.5 m to +1.5 m and measure less than 0.75 m of dry terrain overlap with water deeper than 0.9 m. These are sampled numerical checks, not a proof for every point or a visual review.

The existing simulation does not render a bathymetrically matched exposed seabed for all low-tide shoals; this change does not add or claim that feature. Browser rendering, visual quality, shader compilation on target devices and real GPU performance still need verification.

The first-lesson camera looks toward existing ridge geometry whose median projected depths are approximately 2.4–2.85 km. The former 2 km clear-weather gameplay fog limit fully obscured those median samples. The gameplay limit now reaches 4 km at maximum visibility, blending back to the prior range by visibility 0.72; the passing-shower and minimum-visibility ranges are unchanged. The water receives the same nominal fog limits, although its shader uses radial distance while scene fog uses view-space depth; the camera also clips geometry beyond 3.5 km. A source-level camera/ridge test checks the geometry and fog math without adding triangles or render passes. It does not establish the rendered appearance or device performance.
