# Forecast change exercise

The chart activity starts afloat. At least 150 m from the Juniper visitor berth,
the player receives a forecast of rising wind. After 12 seconds, the scenario
changes the simulated wind from 6 to 10.5 m/s. The player reefs, sails with
attached flow and controlled heel for five seconds, then docks at the existing
Juniper berth. The reef is a demonstrated skill, not a requirement to keep the
same sail area throughout the return; the player can adjust sail as needed.
The existing activity score reflects trim and excessive heel over the attempt,
and the existing docking gates require a slow, aligned arrival. A contact,
grounding, or position discontinuity fails the
attempt; recover and retry. Existing weather settings are restored on exit.
Progress advances when each stage is reached. Route efficiency uses the minimum
straight-line distance to reach the 150 m departure radius and return, or the
initial distance to the berth when starting outside that radius; sailing angles
and harbor approaches can make a longer route necessary.

The timings, force thresholds and distance are game parameters, not a safe
weather limit or a substitute for judgment. A pre-existing correct reef counts;
no arbitrary control movement is required. This exercise does not teach anchor
holding or establish that the actual boat reached safety on shore.

The US National Weather Service advises watching forecasts and returning to
port or safe shelter when threatening weather develops:
https://www.weather.gov/safety/safeboating-during

The exercise requires a full boat-physics, harbor-arrival and browser playthrough,
plus qualified seamanship review, before representing it as verified training.
